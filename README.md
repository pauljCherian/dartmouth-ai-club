# Dartmouth AI Club website

The site at [aiclub.dartmouth.edu](https://aiclub.dartmouth.edu). Plain HTML, CSS and a little JavaScript, generated by a small build script with no dependencies. Vercel builds and hosts it.

## Change the content

The talks, the people, the links and the contact live in `content/`: edit the JSON, commit, push, and Vercel rebuilds the site. The sentences on each page are in the page itself: `src/pages/index.mjs` (the four lines and About), `src/pages/join.mjs`, `src/pages/speakers.mjs` (its lead), `src/pages/404.mjs`, and the footer's invitation in `src/lib/core.mjs`.

| File | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `content/site.json` | Tagline, description for search results, contact email, the club's Dartmouth Groups page (where people join), the club's X and YouTube links, hero photo and caption |
| `content/speakers.json` | Every guest talk: who they are, what they do, when they came. The site sorts them by date |
| `content/team.json` | The officers (name, role, and `linkedin` / `x` profile links), their academic year (`officers_term`), and the faculty advisors (name, school or department). The team page is a list: no photos. It shows the president first, then everyone else by last name, whatever the order in the file |

When the officers change, update `officers_term` and `officers` in `content/team.json` and `contact` (email, name, role) in `content/site.json` in the same commit.

What is not on the site, on purpose: the meeting night and room, a member count, and claims nobody can check. Facts still waiting for an officer's answer are listed on the review page of the preview. The night and room change every term, so /join says they are posted on Dartmouth Groups; a number nobody keeps up to date costs more trust than it earns. The build refuses `meetings`, `members` or `claims` in `site.json`, and refuses placeholder words (TEMP, TBD, “to come”, “to confirm”…) in anything a page shows.

### Add a speaker

Add an entry to `content/speakers.json` (anywhere; the site sorts by date). Every talk shows the same things in the same order: their photo, who they are, what they do, and when they came (the home page leaves out the one line on their work).

```json
{
  "id": "first-last",
  "name": "First Last",
  "role": "Head of Research, Company",
  "description": "One plain line on what they work on, under about 90 characters.",
  "date": "2027-01-19",
  "photo": "first-last",
  "photo_alt": ""
}
```

- `role`: their title and organisation **at the time of the talk**, as "Title, Organisation" ("Head of Data, Simile"), the title in Title Case, with a second affiliation after a semicolon if it matters ("…, Anthropic; PhD Student, Oxford"). Never "Formerly at…": the list is a record of who came, so it never needs updating when people move on. A word too long for a home tile on a phone with large text gets a soft hyphen where it may break, written `\u00ad` ("Meta Super\u00adintelligence Labs"); it is used only on the narrowest screens, so everywhere else the word stays whole.
- `description`: one plain line about the person's own work at the time, under about 90 characters, starting with a verb ("Studies why AI models fail…"). Not their company's slogan, and not the title again. Write it for someone who doesn't study computer science.
- The live build stops while a talk has no `role` or `description`. The preview shows the gap in gray instead, so you can see what is missing.
- `date`: the day of the talk. Add a talk on the day it happens or after: the list is a record of who came, so it never shows a talk as coming up and never needs updating afterwards.
- The home page shows the six most recent talks (photo, name, role, date); `/speakers` shows every talk, grouped by year, with its one line.
- `photo_alt`: leave it `""` for a plain head-and-shoulders portrait (the name is printed right next to it, so screen readers would hear it twice). Describe the photo only when it shows more, such as the speaker at the event.
- `photo_credit` (only for a photo whose licence asks for a credit, e.g. from Wikimedia Commons): `{"by": "Photographer", "source": "https://…", "license": "CC BY-SA 2.0", "license_url": "https://…"}`. The credit line is shown wherever the photo appears; a photo the club took itself needs none.

### Add or replace a photo

The best speaker photo is one the club takes at the talk: the speaker facing the camera, in front of the Dartmouth backdrop (like Yann LeCun's). Replace a borrowed photo when the club has its own, and never flip one (it changes the person's face). Photos go through one script, so every portrait gets the same crop and the same ink-and-paper tone. It needs Python 3 with Pillow and numpy (`pip install pillow numpy`). iPhone originals (`.heic`) also need `pip install pillow-heif`; without it, export the photo as a JPEG at full size.

1. Put the original in `assets-src/people/` (or `assets-src/hero/hero.jpg` for the group photo). Phone photos carry the location where they were taken, and `assets-src/` is committed, so copy them in with the script, which writes an upright JPEG with no metadata at all:
   - a portrait: `python3 tools/images.py original ~/Downloads/IMG_1234.HEIC assets-src/people/first-last.jpg`
   - the group photo: `python3 tools/images.py original ~/Downloads/IMG_0412.HEIC assets-src/hero/hero.jpg --force` (`--force` because it replaces the current one)
2. Add or edit its line in `content/photos.json`. `crop` is a 4:5 box in the original's pixels, `x,y,w,h`: eyes about 40% from the top, head about 55-60% of the box height.
3. Run:

```bash
python3 tools/images.py all
```

That rebuilds every image in `public/img/` in AVIF, WebP and JPEG, and the link-preview image `public/og.jpg` (the group photo beside the logo), never upscales, converts to sRGB, and strips all metadata including location. People without a usable photo are listed under `missing` and get a neutral initials tile on the site. Any other `photo` id without an image stops the build, so a typo can't slip through.

For the group photo, `hero.ceiling` in `content/photos.json` is the fraction of its height, measured from the top, that is only ceiling (0.18 now; the highest head starts at about 0.215). Wide screens crop that much and no more, and get margins beyond it. Measure it again if the photo changes. Crops marked `"frac": true` (none uses it now) are fractions of the image, so they stay right when a larger copy of the same photo replaces it.

`assets-src/brand/og-logo.png` is the logo for `og.jpg`, rendered from `src/svg/logo-display.svg` (ink on transparent, 544px wide); `public/logo-512.png`, the square logo search engines show, is the same lockup 400px wide on paper. Render both again if the logo changes.

## Build and look at it locally

Needs Node 18 or newer. Nothing to install.

```bash
npm run dev
```

That builds `dist/` and serves it at http://localhost:4321, on this computer only, with the same clean URLs, redirects and headers as Vercel. Other commands:

```bash
npm run build      # production build into dist/
npm run preview    # preview into preview/ (inlined CSS, JS and fonts; links end in .html). preview/index.html is a
                   # fragment for the Claude artifact viewer, which supplies the page frame; open the other pages directly.
BUILD_NOW=2027-01-15T12:00:00-05:00 npm run build   # pretend it is another date (the footer's year)
ALLOW_MISSING=1 npm run build      # development only: placeholders for missing fonts and portraits instead of stopping
```

The build stops with a list of problems if content is invalid (including a talk date that doesn't exist, or a field the site no longer uses), a photo or font is missing, or any internal link, anchor, icon or redirect target is broken.

## Deploy

Vercel reads `vercel.json`: build command `node tools/build.mjs`, output `dist/`, clean URLs, and permanent redirects from the old pages (`/meeting-materials` and the old poster address `/images/chetan-patel-poster.png` to `/speakers`; `/resources`, `/reading-lists` and `/reading` to the home page). Every branch pushed to GitHub gets its own preview URL. Merging into `main` publishes to aiclub.dartmouth.edu. Fonts are published under names that include a hash of their contents, so browsers can keep them for a year and still get a replaced font at once.

## Design rules

- **Colour.** Paper `#F4F4F1`, ink `#161616`, gray `#666662`, hairlines `#D9D9D4`. Never pure white or pure black. Dartmouth Green `#00693E` marks what you can press (the Join links and buttons, link hover except the logo, which never changes), the keyboard focus and selected text; nothing else is green.
- **Type.** One family, two weights. Helvetica on Apple devices and TeX Gyre Heros (a free Helvetica clone) everywhere else, through the single family name `Club Sans` in `src/styles/site.css`. Seven sizes, each with one job, set as tokens at the top of `site.css`: page title, menu, heading, lead, body, small, caption. Bold for page titles, people's names, and links and buttons that stand on their own (every list of pages included); regular for what you read, the contact address too. A new size or a new weight is a new rule: add it to the tokens and to the review page's rules, or don't add it.
- **Logo.** The club's name in TeX Gyre Heros Bold, set as two lines and converted to outlines, so it looks identical on every device. Never retype it as live text. There is one drawing, `src/svg/logo-display.svg`: letters 0.045 em tighter than the font, lines 0.81 em apart (a gap of about 8% of the letter height), so the name reads as one block. The giant home title and the header logo (124 px wide on laptops, 108 px on phones) are that same drawing at two sizes, which is why the hand-off can end on exactly what it started as. Never give the header its own spacing: a second drawing makes the letters jump apart at the end of the hand-off.
- **Layout.** A page has one giant title. Below it, every section is the same: a gray hairline from margin to margin, one heading top left (large, regular weight) and the content on the right. The footer is the last section of every page and is laid out the same way: “Join the club.” top left, its sentence and button on the right (on every page but /join), then the page links and the contact. On /join, where the page itself is the invitation, the footer has no heading. A section's content may divide its own width (the speaker tiles: halves on phones, thirds from 700 px). Every hairline sits one section rhythm below what comes before it, the page head included.
- **Arrow.** `src/svg/arrow-e.svg` and `arrow-ne.svg`: → goes somewhere on this site; ↗ leaves it. Arrows go on standalone links and buttons only, at one size (0.62em, the ↗ 0.70em) and 0.4em from the words. A link inside a sentence carries none, takes its sentence's colour and is underlined. A list of pages carries no arrow either; only its current page is underlined. The LinkedIn, X and YouTube marks need no arrow: the mark says where it goes. They are one mark everywhere: ink, as tall as body text is set (17 px on phones, 18 px on laptops), each in its own proportions, in a tap target of about 33 px.
- **Links.** Hover only changes the colour: ink turns green, green turns deeper green, a button's fill turns deeper green. Underline marks the current page in a list of pages, never a hover.
- **Navigation.** Laptops: the logo (home) and Speakers, Team, Join the club. Phones: the menu lists all four pages, Home first, with the club's X and YouTube marks under them once their links are in `content/site.json`. The contact email is in the footer only.
- **Words.** Sentences end with a full stop (“Join the club.”, “Read the paper.”); names don't (“Speakers”, “Team”). One name per thing: a meeting is the club's own session, a talk is a guest's visit. Curly quotes and apostrophes (“ ” ’), an en dash in ranges (2026–27), one space between sentences. The live build stops on a straight quote or apostrophe, or two spaces in a row, in any text a page shows.
- **Error page.** Any unknown address gets `404.html` with status 404: a “Not in service” sign (one ink panel, the site's type in paper, a thin rule along its top), one sentence, one link home; the header and footer carry the rest. It is the only black panel on any page (apart from the skip link when it has focus), because it is the only page that isn't normal browsing. Its words and the rule along its top come from New York subway signs: an open choice on the review page (the recommended version titles it “Page not found”, its name, without the rule).
- **Motion.** One choreographed moment: the hand-off on the home page. Native scrolling, the browser's own cursor, nothing fading in on scroll. People who ask their device for reduced motion get the static layout. The browser plays the hand-off from the scroll position (CSS scroll-driven animations: Chrome, Safari 26 and later, Firefox 159 and later), in step with the finger and, in Chrome and in Safari from 26.4, off the main thread: `site.js` only measures the page and hands the browser the path as keyframes, and checks once that the browser follows it. Older browsers get the same path from `site.js`, frame by frame. Don't move anything in the hand-off from a scroll event: on phones the page scrolls ahead of the script, which then trails the finger and stutters.

## Folders

```
content/        the talks, people, links and contact (edit these)
src/pages/      one file per page
src/lib/        shared header, footer, layout and helpers
src/styles/     site.css
src/scripts/    site.js (hand-off, mobile menu, header rule)
src/svg/        the logo, arrows, and the LinkedIn, X and YouTube marks
public/         copied as-is: fonts, images, icons
tools/          build.mjs, serve.mjs, images.py
assets-src/     metadata-free originals of the photos, and the logo for og.jpg (not published)
```
